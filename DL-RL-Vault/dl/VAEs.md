# VAEs — Variational Autoencoders

**Tags:** #dl #generative #architecture

Generative latent-variable models trained by **maximising a variational lower bound** on the data likelihood. Introduced by Kingma & Welling 2013 ("Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes"). Combine deep autoencoders with probabilistic inference.

## The setting

Assume data is generated by:
1. Sample latent $z \sim p(z)$ (typically $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$ in low dim).
2. Sample data $x \sim p_\theta(x \mid z)$ (a neural net decoder).

The marginal $p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x | z) p(z) dz$ is intractable for any deep decoder, so we can't directly maximise log-likelihood.

## Variational inference + ELBO

Introduce an approximate posterior $q_\phi(z | x)$ (a neural net encoder). The **Evidence Lower BOund (ELBO):**
$$\log p_\theta(x) \;\geq\; \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - \mathrm{KL}\bigl(q_\phi(z|x) \,\|\, p(z)\bigr) \;=:\; \mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; x).$$

**Reading:**
- First term: **reconstruction** — how well does the decoder reproduce $x$ from a sampled $z$?
- Second term: **KL regulariser** — push the encoder's distribution $q(z|x)$ toward the prior $p(z)$.

The gap between $\log p(x)$ and the ELBO is exactly $\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z|x) \| p_\theta(z|x))$, which is $\geq 0$ — hence "lower bound", tight iff $q$ matches the true posterior.

## The reparameterisation trick

To backpropagate through the sampling $z \sim q_\phi(z|x)$, write:
- $q_\phi(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x)))$
- Sample $\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$
- Compute $z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \varepsilon$

Now $z$ is a **deterministic function of $\phi, x, \varepsilon$**, so gradients flow through $\mu$ and $\sigma$. The stochasticity is "pushed outside" the gradient.

(More generally, the reparameterisation trick is a standard technique for differentiating through any continuous distribution.)

## Architecture

```
input x  ──► Encoder ──► (μ(x), log σ²(x))
                              │
                          [sample with reparam trick]
                              │
                              z
                              │
                          Decoder ──► x̂
```

**Loss:**
$$-\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\|x - \hat x\|^2}_{\text{or other recon loss}} + \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\bigl(q(z|x) \| \mathcal{N}(0, I)\bigr).$$

The KL between two diagonal Gaussians has a closed form:
$$\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2 I) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, I)\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i \bigl(\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2 - \log \sigma_i^2 - 1\bigr).$$

## Generation

After training, sample new $x$:
1. $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ (the prior).
2. $\hat x = \mathrm{Decoder}(z)$.

Quality is typically blurrier than [[GANs|GAN]] or [[Diffusion Models|diffusion]] outputs — VAEs minimise expected pixel error, which favors "average" reconstructions. But the latent space is **smooth and interpretable** — interpolating between two $z$'s gives smooth transitions.

## Key variants

### β-VAE (Higgins et al. 2017)

Multiply the KL term by $\beta > 1$:
$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}[\log p(x|z)] - \beta \mathrm{KL}(q(z|x) \| p(z)).$$

Stronger KL → more disentangled latent dimensions (each unit corresponds to a distinct factor of variation). $\beta < 1$ → less regularisation → better reconstruction but messier latents.

### VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al. 2017)

Discrete latent variables via vector quantization. The encoder maps to a continuous vector, snapped to the nearest entry in a learnable codebook. Decoder works on the codebook embeddings.

- Avoids "posterior collapse" (where $z$ is ignored).
- Used in **DALL-E** (text-to-image, autoregressive over VQ-VAE codes), **VALL-E** (audio), **MuseGAN** (music).

### Hierarchical / Deep VAEs (NVAE, very deep VAEs)

Multiple latent levels for richer expressivity. Competitive with diffusion on some benchmarks; harder to train.

### Conditional VAE (CVAE)

Condition encoder and decoder on a label $y$: $q(z|x, y)$, $p(x|z, y)$. Enables controlled generation.

## Posterior collapse

If the decoder is too expressive (e.g. an autoregressive PixelCNN as the decoder), it can model $p(x)$ without using $z$ at all → encoder learns to send $q(z|x) = p(z)$ exactly, KL = 0, $z$ is uninformative.

**Mitigations:** KL annealing (gradually increase $\beta$ from 0), free bits (don't penalise small KL per dim), VQ-VAE (discrete codes can't be ignored), smaller decoder.

## VAEs vs. GANs vs. Diffusion

| | VAE | GAN | Diffusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training | stable (MSE-like) | unstable | stable (MSE) |
| Likelihood | yes (bound) | no | yes (bound) |
| Sample quality | blurry | sharp, lower diversity | sharp, high diversity |
| Inference speed | fast | fast | slow (many denoising steps) |
| Latent space | smooth, useful | implicit, hard to invert | implicit (via diffusion) |
| Mode coverage | strong (full support) | poor (mode collapse) | strong |

## Modern use

VAEs are largely **out of fashion** for image generation but heavily used as:
- **First-stage compressors** for diffusion models (latent diffusion / Stable Diffusion uses a VAE encoder to compress to latent, then diffuses there).
- **VQ-VAE codes** as the input to autoregressive models (DALL-E v1).
- **Representation learning** for downstream tasks.
